# Blog 1: Deactivating my Instagram Account - A Cognitive Walkthrough 

In Monday's lecture (10/21), we applied cognitive walkthroughs to measure the Gulf of Execution – the gap between a user’s goal and the means to execute that goal. Cognitive walkthroughs are guided by four questions: 

How easily can a user... 

1. know they need to do something? 

2. notice the correct action is available, and associate it with what they're trying to do?  

3. based on what occurs after the action is taken, know that it was the right thing to have done?  

4. understand how they've made progress towards their larger goal? 

In this blog, I aim to use a cognitive walkthrough to expose the considerable breadth of the Gulf of Execution when a user intends to deactivate an Instagram account. I can break the larger task – deactivating my Instagram account – into a series of smaller subtasks: logging in, navigating to the appropriate Settings panel, confirming account deactivation. I’ll contrast how Instagram successfully narrows the Gulf in the first subtask with how they widen it in the second two.  

## Logging In

| **_Step_**                     | Description                                                                                                                                                              |
|--------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **_1. Know to do something_**   | **Yes!** Prompts direct me to enter account information into the correct fields.                                                                                        |
| **_2. Notice the correct action_** | **Yes!** The bright blue “Log In” button is the most salient feature on the webpage.                                                                                      |
| **_3. Know it was the right thing_** | **Yes!** The “Log In” button becomes clickable and turns a darker shade of blue when I enter potentially valid credentials. <br><br> The login page includes screenshots of an example user’s Home and Explore pages, like a peek behind the authentication curtain. When I log in, I see the same interface from the screenshots, which shows me that I’ve made progress. |
| **_4. Understand their progress_** | **Yes!** I know I’ve proven account ownership by logging into my Instagram account, and that the same proof authorizes me to deactivate it.                               |

## Navigating to the Appropriate Settings Panel

| **_Step_**                     | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **_1. Know to do something_**   | **Yes!** I see an abundance of categories, and I think I will need to click into the right one to find the panel I am looking for.                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **_2. Notice the correct action_** | **No!** My first instinct is to click into “Account Status,” because I would use the words “activated” and “deactivated” to describe the status of my Instagram account. But the panel I am looking for is not in this menu at all. Instead, I needed to click a link to the Meta Accounts Center. In the Accounts Center, it is equally futile to guess which category contains the option to deactivate my account. |
| **_3. Know it was the right thing_** | **No!** Because the panel to deactivate my Instagram account is so far nested in my account Settings, it was often unclear which parent panels I needed to click into to find it. As I got closer to the child panel, parent panel descriptions included “deactivate or delete account,” but I did not know how many more layers of nesting I would have to enter through. The nesting hierarchy is unintuitive and arbitrary. |
| **_4. Understand their progress_** | **Yes!** I can see the form to deactivate my account.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

## Confirming Account Deactivation

| **_Step_**                     | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
|--------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **_1. Know to do something_**   | **Yes!** The interface holds my hand through the form submission.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **_2. Notice the correct action_** | **Yes!** Buttons to continue through the form are blue, while ones to cancel form submission are white.                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **_3. Know it was the right thing_** | **No!** Depending on the reason I chose to deactivate my account, the form shows me an intermediate screen before asking if I want to deactivate. The intermediate screen contains links to Meta articles offering alternatives to deactivation related to my reason. There is no indicator on this screen that I will still eventually be asked if I want to deactivate. |
| **_4. Understand their progress_** | **No!** If I do successfully deactivate my account, Instagram takes me back to the Login page. Out of instinct or habit, or even to check that my account was successfully deactivated, I might log back in. Once I do that, my account becomes re-activated and I cannot deactivate it again for the next seven days.                          |

## Reflection

To narrow the Gulf of Execution in the second subtask, I suggest a search bar to query settings panels directly, like in Windows Settings, and in the third, any confirmation message. But I doubt that Meta lacks the awareness that clicking through their nested Settings panels is like wandering through a labyrinth. Nor do they lack the resources to simplify their design. The interface is designed to deter users from deactivating their account. If they still manage to do so, returning to the Login page after form submission likely tricks the user into reactivating their account. In these two subtasks, we have observed Meta intentionally widening the Gulf of Execution to discourage an undesirable action that they are otherwise morally obligated to support.
